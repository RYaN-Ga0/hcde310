## Resource 1: Open-Meteo Weather API

**What it provides** (1–2 sentences):
Open-Meteo provides current weather conditions and forecasts, including temperature, humidity, precipitation, and wind speed. Users can request weather data for specific locations using latitude and longitude.

**Docs link:**
https://open-meteo.com/en/docs

**Access:** API key needed? Rate limits? Cost? (write "none" if none)
The free version doesn't require an API key for non-commercial projects. It allows up to 10,000 requests per day, and users need to give credit to Open-Meteo. Commercial use requires a paid plan.

**Sample response** (trim it to a few fields; paste the URL you called too):

URL:

**Who could this data help, and with what?** (2–3 ideas; name real people and a real need, e.g. "first-year students who want to ...")

1.First-year UW students who are unfamiliar with Seattle weather could check upcoming rain and temperature to decide what to wear and whether to bring an umbrella.
2.UW students who walk or bike to campus could use weather forecasts to choose a more comfortable departure time and prepare for rain or strong winds.
3.UW club officers who organize outdoor events could compare forecast conditions at different times to choose a suitable time for a picnic, outdoor meetup, or tabling event.

**One risk** (missing data, bias, privacy, or reliability):
Weather forecasts are estimates and may not accurately reflect conditions at a specific street or building. Localized rain and sudden weather changes may not be captured, so the app should display forecast times and avoid presenting predictions as guarantees.

## Resource 2: iNaturalist Observations API

**What it provides** (1–2 sentences):
The iNaturalist Observations API provides records of organisms observed and submitted by community members. Records can include observation dates, species names, identification status, location information, and links to the original observations.

**Docs link:**
https://www.inaturalist.org/pages/api+reference
**Access:** API key needed? Rate limits? Cost? (write "none" if none)
Public observations can be searched without an API key, and the API is free to use. iNaturalist recommends making about one request per second and keeping requests under 10,000 per day to avoid sending too many requests.

**Sample response** (trim it to a few fields; paste the URL you called too):

**Who could this data help, and with what?** (2–3 ideas)

1.UW students who want to learn about local wildlife could explore observations of different species in parks near campus and follow links to the original records.
2.Seattle families looking for educational outdoor activities could use a species explorer to discover organisms reported in nearby parks and learn what to look for during nature walks.
3.Community-science volunteers who help identify wildlife could find observations marked as needing identification and contribute to the identification process.

**One risk** (missing data, bias, privacy, or reliability):
iNaturalist observations may not represent all species because they depend on where people look. Some records may be incorrectly identified or have hidden locations.
